# GRE-006: Greek given names that are also common nouns or places need context, since case and article cues are weak

## Question and scope

Which Greek given names collide with common nouns or place names, and how reliable are surface cues such as capitalization and the article for telling them apart? Scope: Modern Greek, Grek script, edited prose with all-caps headline contrast, PERSON versus common noun and place. Excludes corpus frequency, rare names and ancient or mythological reading as a separate entity type. It matters because capitalization is lost in all-caps and sentence-initial positions.

## Observation

Wiktionary lists Ζωή as a female given name derived from the word for life (Ancient Greek ζωή), Ελένη as a given name and a mythological name (Helen), and Δάφνη as a female given name, a place name in Athens and elsewhere, and, in Ancient Greek, the word for laurel ([GRE-S010](../sources.md#gre-s010); community-edited, summary read). Fetches for Άνθος, Ελπίδα and Αγάπη returned HTTP 404, so the common-noun status of those words, though known to the author as ordinary vocabulary words (flower, hope, love) that are also used as given names, is unverified in any source here. Modern Greek common-noun senses of Ζωή and Δάφνη were also not separately read.

Cue behavior: the article does not discriminate (η Δάφνη can be the name or the laurel, [GRE-003](GRE-003.md)). Capitalization discriminates in running lowercase-initial text but is lost in all-caps text ([GRE-004](GRE-004.md)). Inflection does not discriminate when forms coincide (Ζωή, Ζωής for both).

Confidence **low**: only three words received any lexical check, and by a community lexicon.

## NER hypothesis

Hypothesis (untested): in Greek, a name gazetteer alone overgenerates on lowercase and all-caps common-noun uses, and local context plus capitalization recovers precision in edited text but not in all-caps text. It is refuted if a gazetteer-only baseline matches a context model on matched name/non-name pairs. On all-caps slices, where capitalization carries no information, it is refuted if context features add nothing over the gazetteer.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: name reading in context. Hard negatives: the same string as a common noun or place. Variation axes: capitalization (sentence-initial, mid-sentence, all-caps), article gender and case, place versus person, mythological reference. Preserve ambiguous contexts as unresolved rather than forcing a label. Destination `ner-evidence`; deferred pending qualified Greek review of examples and an annotation-policy decision. No issue opened.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice `el-name-homograph`: paired items with the same string as name and common noun in matched frames; compare gazetteer-only, capitalization-aware and context-aware conditions; report false positives on common nouns and misses on lowercase-initial names. Destination `ner-eval`; deferred until reviewed evidence exists. No measurement is claimed.

## Implementation options

Candidate experiments: lexicon-plus-context versus case-only; article agreement features. Baseline is the current approach. Decide the PERSON policy for mythological and place-named uses before annotation. Destination `fastner`; options only, nothing adopted.

## Limitations and review

Only three words had any lexical check, and only through a community lexicon; Ελπίδα, Αγάπη, Άνθος, Μύρτος and surnames that are words (such as Παπάς) were not verified. No frequency data. Place/person boundary for Δάφνη depends on policy. No qualified Greek-language review is recorded; the author's self-check of examples is not independent review.
