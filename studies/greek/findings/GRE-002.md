# GRE-002: The Greek feminine surname is genitive in form, so a surname string can be masculine genitive or feminine nominative

## Question and scope

How do Greek surnames and women's surnames relate across case, and what does that do to the identity of a surface string? Scope: Modern Greek, Grek script, edited prose and administrative naming, PERSON. Excludes historical naming law, Cypriot practice, and married-name legal status beyond what the cited pages say. It matters because the same string can be a masculine genitive and a feminine nominative.

## Observation

A reference page on Greek names says women's surnames traditionally use the genitive case of the family name (Yannatos becomes Yannatou) and lists -opoulos ("son of") and -idis among common patronymic-type suffixes ([GRE-S009](../sources.md#gre-s009); secondary, summary read; the page notes modern women typically keep their father's surname). Wiktionary gives for Παπαδόπουλος the etymology παπάδ(ες) plus -όπουλος, a genitive Παπαδόπουλου / Παπαδοπούλου and a feminine form Παπαδοπούλου ([GRE-S010](../sources.md#gre-s010)). The same string Παπαδοπούλου is therefore documented as both a masculine genitive variant and the feminine surname. Inference (author): a person with a feminine surname Παπαδοπούλου has that string as a nominative-role mention, so genitive inference from the ending would be wrong for her.

Counterexample or limit: a feminine -ου surname is not always read as a genitive by native speakers in the sense of case role; here it functions as the fixed feminine surname form. Modern law on married names is not read here; a search snippet pointed to Law 1329/1983 but its text was not read, so no legal claim is made.

Confidence **medium**: the genitive-form convention appears on two secondary pages that are not independent of each other, and no primary grammar or civil-registry text was accessed.

## NER hypothesis

Hypothesis (untested): a model or rule that treats a surname ending in -ου as a genitive modifier only (and excludes it from a following PERSON span) loses feminine full names such as example-01 and example-05; contextual features outperform ending-only rules on these. Refuted if ending-only rules show no loss on a matched set or if context features do not help.
