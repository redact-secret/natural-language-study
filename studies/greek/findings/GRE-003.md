# GRE-003: Greek articles, fused preposition-article forms and abbreviated titles sit next to names as external cues

## Question and scope

Which Greek function words and abbreviations adjacent to a person name belong outside the PERSON span, and how do annotation conventions for tokens relate to that boundary? Scope: Modern Greek, Grek script, edited prose, PERSON. Excludes titles in religious or military titulature, spoken forms and fully informal abbreviation. It matters because Greek puts a definite article before names and fuses prepositions with articles.

## Observation

A Modern Greek grammar overview states that the definite article regularly precedes proper names, with Ο Αλέξανδρος ήρθε χθες as its example ([GRE-S008](../sources.md#gre-s008); secondary, summary read). UD Greek treats the preposition σε plus a definite article as a multiword token split into syntactic words, for example στον = σ + τον, and treats abbreviations as single words that may contain punctuation, for example χλμ. and π.Χ. ([GRE-S001](../sources.md#gre-s001), Tokenization and Word Segmentation; summary read). The UD GDT treebank reports 1,668 multiword tokens in 61,673 tokens and PROPN tagging without NER annotation ([GRE-S002](../sources.md#gre-s002)). These are syntactic annotation conventions, not PERSON span rules.

Author application: because the article and the fused preposition-article carry the case of the name, they are left-context cues that sit outside the name; a fused στον ends in the article's form and is a single orthographic token before the name. The elided preposition with apostrophe (for example απ' τον) was not found documented in the sources read, so its treatment is a gap. Whether titles such as κ. and κα are abbreviations like those UD describes was not sourced; this is an inference from the abbreviation convention.

Confidence **medium**: article-before-name and the fused preposition are documented, but boundary policy is not, and no primary Greek grammar was read.

## NER hypothesis

Hypothesis (untested): treating the definite article, fused preposition-article tokens and abbreviated titles as external left-context cues (features) rather than span members increases exact-span agreement with an exclusion policy, while a model that sees tokens split à la UD gains nothing for PERSON spans. It is refuted if including the article in the span is the stable convention in an adopted annotation policy or if the split gives no boundary change in an evaluated slice.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: article plus name, fused preposition plus name, title plus name, elided preposition. Hard negatives: article plus common noun or adjective (example-05, example-06). Variation axes: case of the article, punctuation of the title, apostrophe character. Destination `ner-evidence`; deferred pending qualified Greek review of examples and an annotation-policy decision. No issue opened.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice `el-article-title-boundary`: hold name inventory fixed and vary article, fused preposition and title; report exact-span errors by cause (article included, name truncated, title included). Destination `ner-eval`; deferred until reviewed evidence exists. No measurement is claimed.

## Implementation options

Compare article-as-feature with article-in-span and UD-split input; keep original text and offsets intact. Title and article inclusion are annotation-policy choices to be fixed downstream first. Destination `fastner`; options only, nothing adopted.
