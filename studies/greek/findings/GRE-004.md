# GRE-004: Greek tonos, final sigma, capitals and Unicode forms give one name several strings

## Question and scope

How do Greek orthography and Unicode representation (monotonic and polytonic spelling, the tonos, final sigma, capitals, and normalization forms) change the strings a PERSON recognizer sees for the same name? Scope: Modern Greek in Grek script; edited and noisy text; PERSON; Unicode 16.0.0 data as reported by Python 3.14.7. Excludes Ancient Greek and liturgical text except where polytonic is named, and fonts. It matters because matching, casing and normalization steps can silently change Greek names.

## Observation

A secondary page reports that monotonic orthography was introduced in 1982 by law, replacing the polytonic system and keeping only the acute tonos for stress and the diaeresis ([GRE-S006](../sources.md#gre-s006); [GRE-S007](../sources.md#gre-s007); secondary, summaries read), and that conservative institutions such as the Greek Orthodox Church still use polytonic spelling. The same pages say sigma has a final form ς at word end and σ elsewhere in both systems, and that in monotonic all-caps text accents are generally omitted while the diaeresis is kept ([GRE-S007](../sources.md#gre-s007)); this last claim is a lead needing an authoritative orthographic source, and the reverse tendency (keeping the tonos on capitals) is attested in Unicode default casing below.

Unicode data (checked via [GRE-S003](../sources.md#gre-s003) summary and locally with Python 3.14.7, Unicode 16.0.0): precomposed ά U+03AC decomposes canonically to α U+03B1 plus U+0301; U+0384 GREEK TONOS has only a compatibility decomposition to U+0020 U+0301, so NFC leaves α followed by U+0384 unchanged and NFKC yields α, space, U+0301; U+1F71 (alpha with oxia) normalizes under NFC to U+03AC; U+037E GREEK QUESTION MARK normalizes under NFC to ASCII U+003B semicolon; U+0387 GREEK ANO TELEIA normalizes to U+00B7. Default full-case lowercasing of ΓΙΩΡΓΟΣ gives γιωργος with final ς U+03C2 (Python), consistent with the Final_Sigma rule in [GRE-S004](../sources.md#gre-s004); default uppercasing of Ζωή gives ΖΩΉ with the tonos retained, which differs from the accentless all-caps convention reported above. The stress position also moves in inflection (Παπαδόπουλος to Παπαδοπούλου, [GRE-S010](../sources.md#gre-s010)), so accent-stripped forms collapse stress variants but also distinct words.

Confidence **medium**: Unicode behavior was verified locally and is stable; orthographic claims rest on secondary pages.

## NER hypothesis

Hypothesis (untested): NFC normalization for lookup, with original offsets preserved, removes NFD and compatibility-tonos (U+0384) variants as an error source; accent folding and final-sigma case handling are separate transformations that help all-caps and headline text. It is refuted if normalized lookup changes no result on a matched NFD set, or if accent folding raises false positives enough to offset recall gains. The prediction concerns names in the evaluated Greek slice, not all-caps text generally.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: NFC, NFD, U+0384 variants, all-caps with and without tonos, polytonic spelling, stress-shifted forms. Hard negatives: accent-folded collisions between a name and an unrelated word. Variation axes: transformation (normalization versus folding), case, register. Destination `ner-evidence`; deferred pending qualified Greek review of examples and an annotation-policy decision. No issue opened.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slices `el-nfc-nfd`, `el-allcaps-accent` and `el-polytonic`, reported separately; compare raw, NFC-with-offsets and accent-folded conditions on the same names. Destination `ner-eval`; deferred until reviewed evidence exists. No measurement is claimed.

## Implementation options

Candidate experiments: NFC lookup with offset mapping; casefold with final-sigma awareness; separate accent-folded key. The no-change baseline is raw bytes. Do not merge accent folding with canonical normalization. Destination `fastner`; options only, nothing adopted.

## Limitations and review

Orthography claims rest on Wikipedia summaries; the Unicode core specification text was not read. All-caps practice in actual Greek newspapers, signs and passports was not measured. Python version-specific behavior (Unicode 16.0.0) may differ elsewhere. No qualified Greek-language review is recorded; the author's self-check of examples is not independent review.
