# JPN-003: Script transitions are cues, not entity boundaries

## Question and scope

When does a script or punctuation change occur inside a name rather than at its edge? Scope: Contemporary standard written Japanese; Hani, Hira, Kana, Latn scripts, general correspondence/narrative, PERSON. Original and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished; see [dossier scope](../overview.md).

## Observation

W3C describes Japanese writing using Han, hiragana, katakana and Latin [JPN-S003](../sources.md#jpn-s003). Unicode distinguishes canonical from compatibility normalization [JPN-S004](../sources.md#jpn-s004). Neither observation makes a script transition a reliable PERSON delimiter; that is the hypothesis to test.

Confidence: **high for the narrowly stated observation**, based on the cited publisher guidance or standard. This confidence does not apply to the NER hypothesis or example interpretations.

Shogakukan’s Digital Daijisen lists both mirror and personal-name readings for ミラー ([JPN-S010](../sources.md#jpn-s010), named entries). This independently supports the lexical collision used below; the constructed sentences still require review.

## NER hypothesis

Script features combined with context will outperform katakana-as-name or script-run boundaries on names and ordinary loanwords. Reject if gains are absent or offset by matched-negative errors.

## Evidence handoff

Pair katakana names/loanwords, Han and kana names, middle-dot variants, Latin plus suffix and fullwidth/halfwidth variants; do not assume transliteration identity. Destination: `ner-evidence`; **deferred pending language/example and annotation-policy review**. No downstream issue has been opened.
