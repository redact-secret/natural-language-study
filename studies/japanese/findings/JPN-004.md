# JPN-004: Within-token boundary capability must be verified before a PERSON model choice

## Question and scope

Can a candidate expose a name boundary inside a script-homogeneous or mixed-kana run? Scope follows the [dossier](../overview.md): contemporary written language, PERSON; instructional examples and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished from domain samples.

## Observation

UD documents multiple Japanese word units ([JPN-S001](../sources.md#jpn-s001), Word Units). Sasano and Kurohashi discuss analyzer-dependent NE boundary conflicts ([JPN-S006](../sources.md#jpn-s006), section 4.2). Misawa et al. motivate character output for within-word boundaries, but their section 5.1 evaluates Product, Location, Organization and Time, **not PERSON** ([JPN-S005](../sources.md#jpn-s005)). That paper supports a representational concern, not a demonstrated PERSON improvement.

Observation confidence: **high** within these source scopes; NER effect remains untested.

## NER hypothesis

Candidates with within-kana-unit endpoints increase the number of reviewed PERSON spans that can be represented relative to fixed unit endpoints. This is a capacity prediction; downstream precision/recall improvement remains a separate hypothesis.

## Evidence handoff

Obtain reviewed kana/Han name variants with and without address suffixes and particles; add pronouns and ordinary loanwords. First ask whether each proposed span is representable, then annotate classification ambiguities. Owner: `ner-evidence`. A concrete [decision brief](../decision-brief.md) is ready for owner review; fixture adoption remains pending linguistic and policy review. No downstream issue opened.

## Evaluation handoff

Propose ja-boundary-representability, before model comparison. The evidence/evaluation owners should count reviewed spans whose start/end are candidate endpoints, with a stated denominator; then compare exact-span precision/recall on identical held-out PERSON data. No imported paper score is a target. Owner: `ner-eval`; proposed slice, not an adopted contract. Model outcomes remain untested.
