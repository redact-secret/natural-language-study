# JPN-005: Romanized name order and readings do not provide a unique name parser

## Question and scope

Which assumptions about name form or reference could reject valid mentions or create false positives? Scope follows the [dossier overview](../overview.md); constructed robustness cases are not claims about normal usage.

## Observation

A 2000 Agency for Cultural Affairs request recommends family-name-first romanization in institutional writing and teaching ([JPN-S007](../sources.md#jpn-s007), page 1). Its date and institutional scope matter: it is not a universal usage claim or an audit of current policy. W3C documents ambiguity between Japanese written forms and readings ([JPN-S009](../sources.md#jpn-s009), Ambiguity in written forms). Inferring a unique identity or name-part division from romanization is therefore unjustified.

Observation confidence: medium overall, because source-backed distinctions are narrower than the proposed NER application.

## NER hypothesis

Generating full surface-name candidates without requiring given-name-first order will avoid an unnecessary rejection gate in mixed Japanese/Latin inputs. Recognition need not recover pronunciation or resolve identity.

## Evidence handoff

Pair source-reviewed name-order variants, kana readings, original written forms and ordinary mixed-script phrases. Preserve separate mentions and document whether parenthesized readings are annotations or prose mentions.
