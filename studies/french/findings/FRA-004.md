# FRA-004: Official particle-casing guidance disagrees, so case must not decide membership

## Question and scope

Can lowercase versus uppercase du/des decide whether a token belongs to a surname? Scope follows the [dossier](../overview.md): contemporary written language, PERSON; instructional examples and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished from domain samples.

## Observation

OQLF illustrates lowercase du/des in personal names ([FRA-S001](../sources.md#fra-s001), Particule nobiliaire de), while the federal Canadian guide recommends uppercase forms ([FRA-S005](../sources.md#fra-s005), Particules ou articles contractés du, des). Both are Canadian editorial authorities; the difference cannot be explained as France versus Québec. The federal guide also says to respect existing names’ spelling. Preserve this disagreement rather than manufacture one universal case rule.

Observation confidence: **high** within these source scopes; NER effect remains untested.

## NER hypothesis

Membership based on context and name components is less sensitive to particle casing than a title-case-only rule, without absorbing external du/des phrases. Reject if casing robustness raises false positives on those controls.
