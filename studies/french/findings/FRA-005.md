# FRA-005: Name-derived common-noun uses require a referent policy

## Question and scope

Which assumptions about name form or reference could reject valid mentions or create false positives? Scope follows the [dossier overview](../overview.md); constructed robustness cases are not claims about normal usage.

## Observation

OQLF describes antonomasia, including proper names used as common nouns, and gives a seducer reading of don Juan ([FRA-S006](../sources.md#fra-s006), Definition and Notes). A name-shaped expression need not directly name an individual. That linguistic distinction does not itself settle a NER taxonomy for figurative references.

Observation confidence: medium overall, because source-backed distinctions are narrower than the proposed NER application.

## NER hypothesis

A referent-aware policy and contextual contrast set will reduce lexical-name false positives relative to a name lexicon alone; the prediction is conditional on an explicit figurative-reference policy.

## Evidence handoff

Have evidence owners distinguish direct reference, fictional-character reference, figurative comparison and lexicalized ordinary nouns. Do not mark every antonomasia automatically non-PERSON, and do not import corpus categories without mapping.

Disposition: ready-for-owner-review local proposal; no downstream issue created.
