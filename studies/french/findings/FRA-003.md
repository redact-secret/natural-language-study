# FRA-003: Compound names and canonical accents must survive matching

## Question and scope

How can compound names and canonically equivalent accents retain correct spans? Scope: Contemporary standard written French; Québec guidance is explicitly scoped; Latn scripts, general correspondence/narrative, PERSON. Original and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished; see [dossier scope](../overview.md).

## Observation

OQLF gives hyphenated personal-name forms [FRA-S001](../sources.md#fra-s001). Unicode canonical equivalence relates accented characters to combining sequences; compatibility normalization is a different operation [FRA-S003](../sources.md#fra-s003). Accent deletion is not NFC and does not establish name identity.

Confidence: **high for the narrowly stated observation**, based on the cited publisher guidance or standard. This confidence does not apply to the NER hypothesis or example interpretations.

## NER hypothesis

Preserving compound-name candidates and canonical-equivalence lookup will improve exact-name consistency versus punctuation splitting, without promoting hyphenated common nouns. Reject if gains are absent or offset by matched-negative errors.

## Evidence handoff

Pair hyphenated names/common nouns and NFC/NFD accents; test accentless and all-caps text as separate lossy transformations, not equivalent gold strings. Destination: `ner-evidence`; **deferred pending language/example and annotation-policy review**. No downstream issue has been opened.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice **fr-compound-normalization: compare hyphen truncation, equivalent-string agreement and original-coordinate validity.** Destination: `ner-eval`; deferred until reviewed evidence exists. Use identical held-out evidence and existing scoring/offset contracts; report slice counts, precision/recall and boundary errors.

## Implementation options

Compare canonical lookup with raw baseline and contextual hyphen joining; make any accent-insensitive lookup a separate precision/recall experiment. Destination: `fastner`; proposed experiments only, deferred until evidence review.

## Limitations and review

Names from other Francophone communities and typography variants need review. No equivalence between accented and unaccented identities is asserted. Independent language review and representative corpus evidence are missing.
