# FRA-002: Elided prepositions can touch a name without belonging to it

## Question and scope

When is d’ external grammar rather than a component of a personal name? Scope: Contemporary standard written French; Québec guidance is explicitly scoped; Latn scripts, general correspondence/narrative, PERSON. Original and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished; see [dossier scope](../overview.md).

## Observation

OQLF describes elision of de before a vowel or mute h, including before proper names [FRA-S002](../sources.md#fra-s002). This differs from name-internal d’ [FRA-S001](../sources.md#fra-s001). French UD also distinguishes orthographic contractions and syntactic words [FRA-S004](../sources.md#fra-s004); syntactic decomposition is not an entity policy.

Confidence: **high for the narrowly stated observation**, based on the cited publisher guidance or standard. This confidence does not apply to the NER hypothesis or example interpretations.

## Illustrative examples and contrasts

Examples: **synthetic**, Codex-authored, fictional people, repository MIT license. Text/translation self-check only; qualified language review pending. Candidate spans are proposals, not gold annotations.

## NER hypothesis

Contextual left-boundary handling will reduce inclusion of elided prepositions without truncating surnames that contain d’. Reject if gains are absent or offset by matched-negative errors.
