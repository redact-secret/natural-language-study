# URD-002: Whitespace is an unreliable word and name boundary in Urdu because of joining behavior

## Question and scope

Can a PERSON candidate be assumed to start and end at whitespace in Urdu, and can a multi-part name be assumed to contain spaces between parts? Scope: Urdu in Pakistan, `Arab` script, typed edited text, PERSON. Excluded: handwriting, Nastaliq rendering, other Pakistani languages, and measured frequency. The answer decides whether candidate generation must look inside or across whitespace-delimited tokens.

## Observation

A word-segmentation paper states that Urdu is written in a cursive Nastalique style that has no concept of space, so a typed space serves other purposes and cannot be a reliable delimiter ([URD-S004](../sources.md#urd-s004), section 1). It describes two problems. Space omission: when a word ends in a non-joining letter, the next word can follow with no space and look the same, so a sentence of such words might contain no space at all (section 2.1). Space insertion: when a morpheme ends in a joining letter, writers may insert a space so the pieces do not join, splitting one word into several tokens (abstract and section 2). The paper introduces an "Orthographic Word" because one such unit may yield several words and several may form one word (section 3). One of its illustrations is a sentence with a person's name, given in translation as "Ahmad Sher Dogar", written both with and without spaces so that the two look visually identical (Table 2); the Urdu is not reproduced here. Its reported test corpus has 2367 words with 404 segmentation errors, of which 221 are omission cases (section 4).

An Urdu NER paper lists space omission and insertion as an unresolved limitation of its rule-based system ([URD-S005](../sources.md#urd-s005), p. 2515, limitation 5). A transliteration paper likewise reports that space is often deleted or extra space added in Urdu words, and treats broken and merged words as a challenge ([URD-S012](../sources.md#urd-s012), section 2). Treebank documentation, by contrast, describes tokens as delimited by whitespace with punctuation separated ([URD-S010](../sources.md#urd-s010)), and the UDTB page reports that 6729 tokens (5 percent) are not followed by a space ([URD-S009](../sources.md#urd-s009)); that figure covers all token types and does not show how many are name-internal.

Confidence: **medium**. The mechanism is documented in three papers from the same research community (two share an institution), so independence is limited; no source measures name-specific splitting.

## NER hypothesis

If a recognizer proposes candidates only on whitespace-token edges, then recall on multi-part Urdu names written with variable internal spacing or ZWNJ will fall below recall on the same names written in the form the training data used; a candidate generator that can start or end inside an orthographic token or span across spaces will reduce the gap without inflating false positives on common-word compounds. The prediction fails if the whitespace baseline shows no recall difference across spacing variants, or if the permissive generator adds more false positives than recovered true positives on matched negatives.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: the same fictional name in no-space, space and ZWNJ forms, both as a bare mention and before `نے`, `کو`, `کا`. Hard negatives: a common compound that happens to resemble a name pair, written with and without a space. Variation axes: spacing, ZWNJ, name length. Preserve invisible characters explicitly. Destination: `ner-evidence`; deferred pending native-speaker review of acceptable spellings.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice **urdu-space-variants**: exact-span precision and recall on matched spacing variants, holding model and split fixed. Report boundary errors (merged with a neighbor, truncated, split into two spans) separately. Destination: `ner-eval`; deferred.

## Implementation options

Whitespace tokenization baseline; character or subword candidates; a segmenter such as the max-matching plus n-gram approach in [URD-S004](../sources.md#urd-s004). Tradeoff: a segmenter adds a dependency and its errors, and its units do not define PERSON spans (UD word units are not PERSON gold). Destination: `fastner`; deferred.

## Limitations and review

No name-specific frequency, no measured effect on any recognizer. [URD-S004](../sources.md#urd-s004) was read as extracted text with garbled Urdu glyphs, and its version identity with Durrani and Hussain (2010) is unverified. Handwritten and Nastaliq-rendered text is outside scope. No qualified Urdu reviewer; author self-check only.
