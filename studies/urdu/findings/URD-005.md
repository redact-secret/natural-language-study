# URD-005: Romanized, Devanagari and mixed-script renderings of Urdu names are variable and not a reversible identity

## Question and scope

How do Urdu names appear when Urdu script is mixed with Latin text, written in Roman Urdu, or transliterated to and from Devanagari, and what does that imply for span detection and matching? Scope: Urdu in Pakistan, `Arab` and `Latn` (`ur-Latn` for romanized contrasts only), PERSON. Devanagari Hindi is described only as the neighboring transliteration target; no Hindi dossier was available to compare, and no Hindi claim is made. Excluded: Roman Urdu as an independent corpus, speech, and other Pakistani languages.

## Observation

Roman Urdu has no standard spelling: one paper lists five spellings of the word for "life" (zindagi, zindagee, zindagy, zaindagee, zndagi), notes spellings that match English words, and notes frequent Roman Urdu and English code-switching ([URD-S011](../sources.md#urd-s011), introduction and problem description). That paper studies common words, not names, so name variation is an extrapolation. An Urdu-to-Devanagari transliteration paper treats the two as variants of one spoken language in different scripts and lists as hard problems the missing diacritics and short vowels in Urdu, characters with zero or multiple Devanagari mappings, word-level ambiguity (one Urdu word, several Hindi words), and word segmentation ([URD-S012](../sources.md#urd-s012), abstract and section 2). Lossless script mapping is therefore not claimed even for common words.

For mixed Urdu and Latin text, the bidirectional algorithm assigns Arabic letters the right-to-left class AL, and neutral characters and punctuation take the direction of surrounding strong text when both sides agree, otherwise the paragraph direction ([URD-S002](../sources.md#urd-s002), sections 3.2, 3.3.5 rules N1 and N2). This affects display order, not the stored order in which spans are indexed. The national identity card prints names in both Urdu and English ([URD-S016](../sources.md#urd-s016)), so both renderings of one person can occur in related documents.

Confidence: **medium** for the mechanisms; **low** for any claim about names specifically, since none of the sources examines name variants.

## NER hypothesis

If an Urdu recognizer is evaluated only on `Arab` strings, its recall on Roman Urdu or Latin-script renderings of the same names in Urdu-context sentences will be lower, and exact string equality between Urdu-script and romanized renderings will fail as a coreference or deduplication cue; a variant-aware matcher will help matching but must not be used to assign gold identity. The prediction fails if Latin renderings are recognized as well as Urdu-script ones, or if a matcher's merges produce false identity links in matched negatives.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: a fictional name in `Arab`, in Latin (several variants), and in both inside one sentence. Hard negatives: Roman Urdu common words that look like names (example-06 style), Latin acronyms. Variation axes: spelling variant, diacritics, script run position relative to postpositions. Record bidi control characters explicitly if present. Destination: `ner-evidence`; deferred.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice **urdu-script-variants**: hold name, context and model fixed; vary script and spelling; report boundary errors at Latin-to-Urdu transitions separately from missed mentions. Destination: `ner-eval`; deferred.

## Implementation options

No-change baseline; character-level candidate generation across script runs; a phonetic-key matcher such as the UrduPhone approach described in [URD-S011](../sources.md#urd-s011) for matching only (not for spans). Tradeoff: phonetic keys merge distinct names. Destination: `fastner`; deferred.

## Limitations and review

No name-specific Roman Urdu or Devanagari data was found. The Hindi side is not compared. The sociolinguistic framing of Hindi and Urdu as one language is the cited authors' and is not endorsed here beyond the shared-register observation. No qualified reviewer.
