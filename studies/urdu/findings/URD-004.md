# URD-004: Pakistani Muslim naming is not a single given-plus-family template, and title versus name-part status needs a policy

## Question and scope

What name components appear in Urdu-language text about people in Pakistan, and which of them (titles, honorifics, caste or tribal names, patronyms, relational names) are ambiguous between name part and title? Scope: Urdu text, `Arab` script, Muslim-majority naming in Pakistan as described by the cited sources, PERSON. Other Pakistani naming communities (non-Muslim Pakistanis, Pashtun, Baloch, Sindhi, Punjabi-specific conventions) are not covered by these sources and no claim is made about them.

## Observation

A linguistics study of Muslim personal names in Urdu, known to this dossier only through a search-result summary because the article pages returned HTTP 403, reports name types of one to four parts, with additional parts for honorific titles, caste titles, patronyms and, for married women, husbands' names, and treats Abdul and forms ending in -ul or -ur as titles rather than personal names ([URD-S013](../sources.md#urd-s013)). This is a lead, not a verified source reading. A second source, an abstract-only reading, frames Pakistani Muslim names as markers of identity, religiosity and class with observed trends of Islamization, Arabization and Westernization ([URD-S014](../sources.md#urd-s014)). A newspaper review of the same author's book reports that Pakistani naming lacks standardized conventions, varies by class, ethnicity and region, and that a husband's or father's first name increasingly serves as a surname ([URD-S015](../sources.md#urd-s015)). The national identity card, per an encyclopedia page, prints given and family name in Urdu and English, plus a father's name or, for married women, a husband's name ([URD-S016](../sources.md#urd-s016)).

For recognition, one Urdu NER system uses a "Title Person" class (for example Mr.), 34 title persons and 102 designations as cues, a gazetteer of about 6000 person names, and a rule limited to three-word names that, by its own account, splits a four-part name into two entities ([URD-S005](../sources.md#urd-s005), p. 2508 Table 1; p. 2511 for the 34 title persons and 102 designations; p. 2515 limitation 1; p. 2516 for the 6000-name gazetteer). Title Person is a separate tag from Person Name in the IJCNLP-08 tag set that paper reproduces.

Confidence: **low**. The two key naming sources were only seen in abstract or summary form; the identity-card source is encyclopedic; no primary National Language Authority or NADRA naming guidance was located. The title/name-part classification therefore stands as a hypothesis space rather than a finding.

## NER hypothesis

Because Urdu name parts vary in number and role, a recognizer trained on two-part given-plus-family names will truncate or over-extend longer names and mislabel title-like parts; policy-explicit variants (titles inside versus outside the span) will make a recognizer's boundary errors measurable instead of ambiguous. The prediction fails if exact-span F1 does not differ between mononym, two-part and four-part names at matched context, or if title inclusion changes nothing for a model.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: mononyms, two-part and four-part names, names with `محمد` or `عبد` first parts, female forms with `بی بی` and `بیگم`, relational forms. Hard negatives: `ملک` as country, `شاہ` as a given name part versus honorific. Variation axes: part count, honorific position (before or after), title inclusion. The title policy (include or exclude) must be set downstream. Destination: `ner-evidence`; deferred pending primary naming sources and native-speaker review.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice **urdu-name-structure**: exact-span precision and recall by part count and title policy; report truncation and over-extension. Destination: `ner-eval`; deferred.

## Implementation options

Gazetteer of name components plus title cues (as in [URD-S005](../sources.md#urd-s005)); a model-first baseline; a hybrid. Avoid hard-coded maximum name length (the three-word rule is a documented failure). Destination: `fastner`; deferred.

## Limitations and review

Key naming sources are summaries; no primary authority; Muslim-majority scope only; no data on name-part frequency. Rahman's work concerns names, not Urdu text mentions. No qualified reviewer.
