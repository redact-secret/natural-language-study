# SPA-006: Common-noun and place homographs of Spanish given names need context, not a name lexicon alone

## Question and scope

Which surface forms such as Pilar, Rosa, Dolores, Mercedes, Jesús, Cruz, Guerra and Mármol collide with common nouns, places or other entities? Scope: written Spanish, PERSON versus non-person readings. Excludes frequency claims.

## Observation

No dictionary entry was verified: the RAE dictionary returned HTTP 403, so the lexical homograph facts below are the author's general-knowledge leads and are unsourced. The sources in this finding support only the surrounding annotation context. The CoNLL-2002 Spanish data are EFE newswire annotated as non-recursive, with only the top-level entity marked when entities nest ([SPA-S013](../sources.md#spa-s013)), so an embedded person inside an organization or place is not separately marked there. UD asks that names lacking internal structure use a flat relation ([SPA-S011](../sources.md#spa-s011)), which is a syntactic convention, not an ambiguity resolution. Observation: no source here quantifies ambiguity. Leads to verify: pilar (column), rosa (color or flower), dolores (pains), mercedes (favors), cruz (cross), guerra (war) are also common words; Jesús and Mármol may be given name, surname, or other entities.

Confidence **low**: no verified lexical or corpus source; this finding mainly fixes the contrast set.

## Illustrative examples and contrasts

All **synthetic**, fictional contexts; author self-check only.

| ID | Original text | Meaning | Boundary question / contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| example-01 | `Pilar llamó a su hermano.` | Pilar called her brother. | Person reading, sentence-initial. |
| example-02 | `El pilar del puente cedió.` | The bridge pillar gave way. | Common noun, lowercase. |
| example-03 | `Pilar no cedió.` | Ambiguous: Pilar or a pillar did not give way. | Sentence-initial capital with no cue; reading not decidable here. |
| example-04 | `Calle Mármol, número 4` | Mármol Street, number 4. | Surname-like word inside an address; not a PERSON. |
| example-05 | `Mercedes llegó en un Mercedes.` | Mercedes arrived in a Mercedes (car). | Person and product with same string. |

## NER hypothesis

Hypothesis (untested): a lexicon of given names without context overpredicts PERSON on example-02, -04 and the car reading in example-05; a context window plus casing reduces these errors. Refuted if lexicon-only performs equally on matched negatives.
