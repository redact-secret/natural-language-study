# SPA-001: Spanish double surnames may carry optional de, y or i connectors, so surname count is not fixed

## Question and scope

Can a PERSON recognizer assume a given name plus one surname, or a fixed two-component surname, for Spanish? Scope: written Spanish, es-ES (state law) and es-AR (civil code on marital use), edited and administrative registers, PERSON, Latn. Excludes Catalan, Galician and Basque naming practice, Mexico (no source gathered), historical naming and identity linking.

## Observation

Spanish civil-registry law lets parents agree the order in which their first surnames are transmitted, and permits the preposition de and the conjunctions y or i to appear between surnames ([SPA-S006](../sources.md#spa-s006), Article 49; fetched as a summary). Argentine law lets either spouse use the other's surname with or without de ([SPA-S007](../sources.md#spa-s007), Article 67; fetched as a summary). The RAE treats the first surname as the axis for ordering lists and disregards a leading de, del or de la there ([SPA-S002](../sources.md#spa-s002); search excerpt only). Taken together: a written person name can contain a given-name sequence, two surnames in either parental order, an optional connector between them, and possibly a marital de-surname. The count of capitalized tokens does not fix how many surnames there are.

Author interpretation, not sourced: the same string `María Pérez de Gómez` is structurally ambiguous between a double surname `Pérez de Gómez` (connector) and a marital use of `Gómez` (the Argentine rule); the text alone may not decide.

Confidence **medium**: two official legal texts agree that connectors exist, but both were read via page summaries and neither describes how often they appear in text.

## NER hypothesis

Hypothesis (untested): a span policy that allows a lowercase de, y or i between two capitalized surname-like tokens, conditioned on local context, recovers full double-surname spans better than a capitalized-run rule, without merging coordinated people as in example-02. Refuted if matched coordination negatives are merged at a rate that offsets the recall gain, or if accepting `y` adds no benefit over the baseline.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: example-01, -03, -04. Hard negatives: example-02 and -05. Variation axes: order of surnames, presence of connector, compound given name, married-name `de`, sentence-initial position. Destination `ner-evidence`; deferred pending language review. No downstream issue opened.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice `es-double-surname-connector`: exact-span precision and recall on connector names versus coordination negatives, identities held out across splits. Report omitted-surname and over-merge errors separately. Destination `ner-eval`; deferred until reviewed evidence exists.

## Implementation options

No-change baseline versus a contextual rule for lowercase `de`/`y`/`i` between capitalized tokens versus learned boundary features. Tradeoff: `y` is a common coordinator, so a lexical rule overgenerates. Destination `fastner`; no work opened.

## Limitations and review

Legal texts describe what may be registered, not usage. Mexico, other Latin American countries, and Iberian co-official languages are not covered, and nothing here measures frequency. Author self-check only; no qualified review.
