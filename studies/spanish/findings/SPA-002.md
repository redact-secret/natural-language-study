# SPA-002: Spanish surname particles are lowercase after a given name and capitalized without it, so case cannot decide membership

## Question and scope

Does the casing of de, del and de la tell a recognizer whether the particle belongs to a surname? Scope: normative written Spanish (RAE, pan-Hispanic), PERSON, edited prose. Excludes individual legal spellings and non-RAE editorial traditions.

## Observation

Search excerpts of RAE guidance report that a preposition, or preposition plus article, beginning a surname is written lowercase when the given name precedes it (`Irene de la Rosa`) and capitalized when the given name is omitted (`señor De Torres`, `De la Rosa`) ([SPA-S001](../sources.md#spa-s001)). RAE alphabetization guidance adds that these particles are disregarded in ordering and written lowercase after the given name (`Torre Ibarra, Ramón de la`) ([SPA-S002](../sources.md#spa-s002)). The same surname therefore appears with two casings depending on whether a given name is present. Neither page was fetched (HTTP 403); this finding rests on excerpts. Whether published text follows the norm is not established, and individuals may fix their own spelling.

Confidence **medium**: authoritative source, but excerpt-only access and normative rather than observed behavior.

## Illustrative examples and contrasts

All **synthetic**, fictional people, repository MIT terms; author self-check only.

| ID | Original text | Meaning | Boundary question / contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| example-01 | `Irene de la Rosa abrió la sesión.` | Irene de la Rosa opened the session. | Lowercase particles inside the name; whole span. |
| example-02 | `El señor De la Rosa abrió la sesión.` | Mr De la Rosa opened the session. | Capitalized particle after a title, given name omitted. |
| example-03 | `Hablé con De la Rosa ayer.` | I spoke with De la Rosa yesterday. | Name only, sentence-internal; capitalized `De la` is name-internal. |
| example-04 | `De la mañana a la noche trabajó.` | From morning to night he worked. | Sentence-initial `De la` is a function phrase, not a name. |
| example-05 | `La mujer de la Rosa Dorada llegó.` | The woman of the Golden Rose arrived. | Lowercase `de la` followed by a capitalized word, external to a non-person name. |

## NER hypothesis

Hypothesis (untested): treating a particle as name-internal from local context (adjacent title or given name, following capitalized surname) outperforms a rule that requires either uppercase or lowercase. Refuted if a case-agnostic contextual rule adds false positives on example-04 and -05 style negatives without gaining on example-02 and -03.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: example-01 to -03. Negatives: -04, -05. Variation axes: title present, given name present, sentence-initial. Destination `ner-evidence`; deferred pending review and verification of the RAE pages.
