# SPA-003: The contractions del and al are external boundaries unless El is part of a name; nicknames differ

## Question and scope

How do the contractions del (de + el) and al (a + el) interact with person-name boundaries? Scope: written Spanish, es-ES newswire and web-genre treebanks plus RAE orthography, PERSON. Excludes speech.

## Observation

Universal Dependencies for Spanish treats al and del as multiword tokens that split into a preposition and an article ([SPA-S008](../sources.md#spa-s008), Tokenization and Word Segmentation); the AnCora and GSD treebank pages report 12,557 and 8,236 multiword tokens respectively, including del and al ([SPA-S009](../sources.md#spa-s009), [SPA-S010](../sources.md#spa-s010); figures from page summaries). That is a syntactic annotation convention and not a PERSON span rule. RAE orthography reportedly says the contraction is not written when El is a capitalized part of a proper name (`a El Salvador`, `de El País`), whereas for nicknames such as El Greco the article is lowercase and contracts (`al Greco`) ([SPA-S014](../sources.md#spa-s014); search excerpt only). In a surname that begins with `del` (a particle fused with the article), `del` is inside the name. Consequence: the same string `del X` can be an external contraction, an internal surname particle, or an article-plus-nickname boundary.

Confidence **medium**: tokenization facts are documented; the RAE rule is excerpt-only and normative.

## Illustrative examples and contrasts

All **synthetic**, fictional people; author self-check only.

| ID | Original text | Meaning | Boundary question / contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| example-01 | `Pedro del Amo llegó.` | Pedro del Amo arrived. | `del` internal to the surname; whole span. |
| example-02 | `El libro del Amo llegó.` | The owner's book arrived. | `del` external; `Amo` is a common noun here. |
| example-03 | `Hablamos con el hijo del Pinto.` | We spoke with the son of the Pinto (a nickname). | Contracted article before a nickname; nickname span is Pinto or `el Pinto`? Policy question. |
| example-04 | `Fue una visita a El Pinar.` | It was a visit to El Pinar. | Place with capitalized El; no contraction written; not a PERSON. |
| example-05 | `Se lo dije a Lucía.` | I told it to Lucía. | `a` external before a name; see [SPA-004](SPA-004.md). |

## NER hypothesis

Hypothesis (untested): UD word splitting of del or al should not change PERSON span membership; a model given split units needs separate handling for internal `del` (example-01) versus external `del` (example-02). Refuted if a UD-style split with no extra context performs equally on both families.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: example-01. Hard negatives: -02, -04. Open policy: nickname with article (example-03). Destination `ner-evidence`; deferred.

## Implementation options
