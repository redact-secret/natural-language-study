# SPA-004: Titles and the personal a are left-context cues outside the PERSON span, with exceptions

## Question and scope

Are abbreviated treatments (Sr., D., Dña., Lic.) and the personal preposition a useful cues that sit outside a name span? Scope: written Spanish, es, PERSON, edited prose. Excludes forms of address in speech and exhaustive treatment inventories.

## Observation

RAE guidance reportedly says abbreviations of treatment formulas take an initial capital (`Sr.`, `D.`, `Ud.`) ([SPA-S003](../sources.md#spa-s003); excerpt only; `Dña.` and `Lic.` not seen there). The RAE grammar glossary reportedly describes direct objects with an animate, specific referent as usually marked with a, while non-specific animate referents are typically unmarked ([SPA-S005](../sources.md#spa-s005); excerpt only). The a is also the dative marker, so it does not identify a direct object by itself. UD notes that abbreviations such as etc. keep their period as part of the token ([SPA-S008](../sources.md#spa-s008)), which means a title abbreviation with its period is a single token, not name material. Author interpretation (unsourced): the title and a are conventionally outside the person's name, but a recognizer may use them as classification cues.

Confidence **low**: three excerpt-level sources; no frequency or disagreement data, and the policy of including titles is a downstream annotation question.

## Illustrative examples and contrasts

All **synthetic**, fictional people; author self-check only.

| ID | Original text | Meaning | Boundary question / contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| example-01 | `El Sr. Marín visitó a Lucía Soto.` | Mr Marín visited Lucía Soto. | `Sr.` cue before name; `a` personal before name; both proposed outside the span. |
| example-02 | `Visitó a la Soto.` | He visited the Soto woman. | Article before a surname; policy question. |
| example-03 | `Escribió a Marín.` | He wrote to Marín. | `a` here is dative; same surface cue, different role. |
| example-04 | `A Coruña tiene puerto.` | A Coruña has a port. | Place name beginning with `A`; the initial `A` is part of the name. |
| example-05 | `Busco a Lucía y busco un fontanero.` | I am looking for Lucía and for a plumber. | Personal `a` before a name versus unmarked non-specific noun. |

## NER hypothesis

Hypothesis (untested): treating title abbreviations and personal a as feature cues, not span members, improves PERSON precision without changing span boundaries. Refuted if removing these cues does not reduce precision, or if the `A Coruña` style negatives (example-04) produce false positives from the cue.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: example-01, -05. Negatives: -03, -04. Policy question: whether titles are included in the span; it must be decided before gold annotation. Destination `ner-evidence`; deferred.
