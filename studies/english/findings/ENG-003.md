# ENG-003: Initials, titles and multipart names cross token boundaries

## Question and scope

How should candidates span initials and name components while handling adjacent titles? Scope: Contemporary edited British and American English; Latn scripts, general correspondence/narrative, PERSON. Original and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished; see [dossier scope](../overview.md).

## Observation

English punctuation guidance uses periods for initials and abbreviations [ENG-S002](../sources.md#eng-s002). W3C describes differing name orders, multiple name components and initial conventions [ENG-S003](../sources.md#eng-s003). English-language text is not limited to one naming community.

Confidence: **high for the narrowly stated observation**, based on the cited publisher guidance or standard. This confidence does not apply to the NER hypothesis or example interpretations.

## Illustrative examples and contrasts

Examples: **synthetic**, Codex-authored, fictional people, repository MIT license. Text/translation self-check only; qualified language review pending. Candidate spans are proposals, not gold annotations.

| ID | Original text | Meaning | Boundary question / contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ex-01 | `Dr. Nora Q. Vale spoke.` | Doctor Nora Q. Vale spoke. | Propose Nora Q. Vale, with internal periods; title inclusion awaits evidence policy. |
| ex-02 | `Nora de Silva spoke.` | Nora de Silva spoke. | Lowercase internal name component must not automatically terminate a name. |
| ex-03 | `Read section Q. Then continue.` | Read section Q; then continue. | A letter followed by a period is not sufficient for PERSON. |

## NER hypothesis

Candidates spanning initials and internal lowercase components will improve exact name recall over title-case token runs, subject to hard-negative precision. Reject if gains are absent or offset by matched-negative errors.

## Evidence handoff

Vary initial position, abbreviation punctuation, titles and multipart surnames; include section letters and organization names. Destination: `ner-evidence`; **deferred pending language/example and annotation-policy review**. No downstream issue has been opened.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice **en-name-components: measure truncation and title over-inclusion separately from identity classification.** Destination: `ner-eval`; deferred until reviewed evidence exists. Use identical held-out evidence and existing scoring/offset contracts; report slice counts, precision/recall and boundary errors.

## Implementation options

Compare contextual multi-token candidates with baseline; permit internal punctuation but constrain using context rather than fixed name length. Destination: `fastner`; proposed experiments only, deferred until evidence review.

## Limitations and review

No universal given/family-name parser is justified. Compound-name and mononym coverage remains incomplete; a title alone is not necessarily a named entity. Independent language review and representative corpus evidence are missing.
