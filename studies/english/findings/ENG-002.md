# ENG-002: Capitalization is a contextual cue, not a PERSON test

## Question and scope

When does capitalization distinguish a personal name from a common noun or other proper noun? Scope: Contemporary edited British and American English; Latn scripts, general correspondence/narrative, PERSON. Original and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished; see [dossier scope](../overview.md).

## Observation

Edited English capitalizes sentence starts, personal names, places, weekdays and other proper nouns [ENG-S002](../sources.md#eng-s002). Therefore capitalization is compatible with several non-PERSON readings. Lowercase and all-caps variants below are deliberate stress inputs, not a claim about their prevalence.

Confidence: **high for the narrowly stated observation**, based on the cited publisher guidance or standard. This confidence does not apply to the NER hypothesis or example interpretations.

Cambridge also lists ordinary noun and verb readings of rose ([ENG-S007](../sources.md#eng-s007), noun/verb entries), supporting the lexical side of the constructed contrast below.

## NER hypothesis

Adding lexical and contextual evidence to casing will improve discrimination of name/common-noun pairs; measure whether gains persist when casing is removed. Reject if gains are absent or offset by matched-negative errors.
