# ENG-004: Joint possession does not merge the people mentioned

## Question and scope

Should a possessive coordinated phrase form one PERSON span or several mentions? Scope follows the [dossier](../overview.md): contemporary written language, PERSON; instructional examples and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished from domain samples.

## Observation

Purdue permits joint possession marked only on the final noun; the Canadian Translation Bureau distinguishes shared from individual possession ([ENG-S005](../sources.md#eng-s005), Forming Possessives; [ENG-S006](../sources.md#eng-s006), Two nouns). These independent editorial sources agree on the construction. Our NER inference is separate: a shared possessed object does not turn two people into one personal name.

Observation confidence: **high** within these source scopes; NER effect remains untested.

## NER hypothesis

A coordination-aware candidate/decoder constraint reduces two-person merges compared with unrestricted name-chain joining, without increasing missed multipart names. Reject if merge reductions come only from truncating legitimate names.
