# KOR-005: Romanized personal names admit variation that a single transliterator cannot define

## Question and scope

Which assumptions about name form or reference could reject valid mentions or create false positives? Scope follows the [dossier overview](../overview.md); constructed robustness cases are not claims about normal usage.

## Observation

The NIKL romanization guidance allows a hyphen within a given name, specifies family-name-first ordering in its standard, and permits established spellings of proper names. It also distinguishes ordinary pronunciation-based transcription from special reversible transcription. See [KOR-S006](../sources.md#kor-s006), Special Provisions (4), (7), (8). A standard spelling generator therefore cannot serve as an exhaustive name validator.

Observation confidence: medium overall, because source-backed distinctions are narrower than the proposed NER application.

## NER hypothesis

Allowing reviewed romanized name forms and name-internal hyphens will improve mention coverage over a single-output romanization whitelist. This does not predict identity-linking accuracy.

## Evidence handoff

Include standard and established spellings with provenance, name-internal versus external punctuation, and non-person romanized text. Keep individual mentions separate; do not manufacture identity equivalence from transliteration.

## Limitations and review
