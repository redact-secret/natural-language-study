# KOR-004: Name-forming 이 and subject-marker 이 require different boundary policies

## Question and scope

Does every 이 after a personal name denote removable grammatical material? Scope follows the [dossier](../overview.md): contemporary written language, PERSON; instructional examples and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished from domain samples.

## Observation

The commissioned teaching grammar distinguishes subject-marker 이/가 from name-attached suffix 이, including a suffix followed by 가 or 도 ([KOR-S003](../sources.md#kor-s003), printed pp. 152–154). Its name restrictions are pedagogical scope, not a census of usage. It does not prescribe our PERSON annotation. The 2022 NIKL-commissioned NER guideline explicitly **includes name suffix 이, excludes case-marker 이, and treats 이랑 as an external particle** ([KOR-S004](../sources.md#kor-s004), guideline p. 57 / PDF p. 149). The inspected `ner-evidence` taxonomy **0.2.0 instead excludes this informal name suffix** ([KOR-S005](../sources.md#kor-s005), line 57). This is a verified policy mismatch, not a contradiction about whether the suffix exists.

Observation confidence: **medium** within these source scopes; NER effect remains untested.
