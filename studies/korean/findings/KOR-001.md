# KOR-001: Attached particles require boundaries within an eojeol

## Question and scope

Where should a name end when particles attach inside the same eojeol? Scope: Contemporary South Korean standard written Korean; Hang, Hani, Latn scripts, general correspondence/narrative, PERSON. Original and constructed robustness inputs are distinguished; see [dossier scope](../overview.md).

## Observation

South Korean orthography attaches particles to preceding forms, including particle sequences (article 41) [KOR-S001](../sources.md#kor-s001). A whitespace-delimited eojeol may therefore contain both a name and following grammar. This does not establish that the preceding noun is a person.

Confidence: **high for the narrowly stated observation**, based on the cited publisher guidance or standard. This confidence does not apply to the NER hypothesis or example interpretations.

## NER hypothesis

Context-sensitive particle boundary candidates will reduce suffix over-inclusion versus whitespace-only candidates, without treating all particle-bearing nouns as names. Reject if gains are absent or offset by matched-negative errors.
