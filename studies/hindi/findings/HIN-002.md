# HIN-002: Honorific श्री and जी are separate words unless part of the name, and annotation schemes disagree on including them

## Question and scope

Are honorific and title tokens (श्री, श्रीमती, जी, डॉ.) inside or outside a Hindi PERSON span, and what does the spelling standard say about when such a token is part of the name itself? Scope: standard written Hindi in Devanagari (hi-IN) and romanized code-mixed tweets (hi-Latn), PERSON only. Not covered: other Indian languages, Urdu-script text, religious or royal titles beyond the examples. The question matters because three attested annotation designs treat the same pattern differently, so span-level agreement cannot be assumed without a stated mapping.

## Observation

The Central Hindi Directorate standard treats श्री and जी as particles (avyaya) that are written as separate words, with illustrations श्री गंगाधर, कन्हैयालाल जी and महात्मा जी. It adds that when श्री or जी is itself part of the proper noun, it is written joined, with illustrations श्रीराम and रामजी लाल [HIN-S001](../sources.md#hin-s001), section 3.5.2-3.5.3, printed p. 28 (examples decoded from an extraction with font artifacts). So the spacing alone separates an honorific from a name-internal element, but only by the writer's convention.

Three annotation designs differ. The IJCNLP NER shared task slides describe a twelve-class tagset with NEP for person and a separate NETP class for person titles (the slide examples include Mr., Shri and Mahatma), and describe maximal-entity manual annotation without nesting in manual annotation while systems must mark nested entities [HIN-S007](../sources.md#hin-s007). The Roman-script Hindi-English tweet corpus annotates `modi/B-Per ji/I-Per`, putting ji inside the Per span [HIN-S008](../sources.md#hin-s008), section 3.1. HiNER follows CoNLL-2003 guidelines [HIN-S005](../sources.md#hin-s005), section 3; the sections read state no explicit rule for honorifics.

A tertiary overview lists -ji, Shri and Smt as common respect markers in North India and treats -ji as a suffix [HIN-S013](../sources.md#hin-s013). That source is a lead only and does not corroborate the standard.

Confidence is **medium** for the spacing rule (one official standard) and for the existence of divergent annotation choices (two read sources, one summarized slide deck). It is not a claim about usage frequency.

## NER hypothesis

A mapping step that assigns honorific tokens to a declared policy (inside, outside, or separate class) per source will reduce spurious boundary disagreement across Hindi corpora more than a single hard-coded rule, and a title-stripper keyed on the lexical form श्री will damage name-internal श्री (ex-03). It would be challenged if exact-span agreement across the three source designs is already high, or if a lexical title-stripper shows no name-internal damage on a matched slice. This concerns boundaries; it says nothing about how often honorifics occur.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: title plus name, name plus जी, name-internal श्री/जी. Hard negatives: honorific tokens with no person (for example a place name beginning with श्री), common words. Contrast families: Devanagari versus Roman jī, spaced versus joined. Destination: `ner-evidence`; deferred and no issue opened. An explicit policy choice belongs to the evidence owner.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice `hi-honorific-policy`: score the same model output against each declared span policy and report the difference; do not average policies together. Failure condition: no difference between policies on a matched slice. Destination: `ner-eval`; deferred.

## Implementation options

Baseline: no honorific rule. Option: configurable title lexicon applied after the model, off by default; option: train with a title class. Tradeoff: spacing is writer-dependent, so lexical rules need contrast tests. Destination: `fastner`; deferred.

## Limitations and review

The IJCNLP source is a slide deck read as extracted text; the full guidelines were not located. HiNER's treatment of honorifics is unknown from the sections read. Muslim, Sikh and other community honorifics (for example साहब, सिंह as part of a name) are not covered. No qualified review.
