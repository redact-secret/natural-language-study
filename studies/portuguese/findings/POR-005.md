# POR-005: Titles and honorifics such as Sr., Dr., Dra., Dona, Seu and Dom have mixed casing status and need an explicit span policy

## Question and scope

How are titles and forms of address written beside Portuguese personal names, and how does their casing and abbreviation affect whether they belong in a PERSON span? Scope: pt-BR institutional guidance and the Acordo; pt-PT usage not independently sourced. Excludes religious and monarchic titles beyond Dom.

## Observation

- The Acordo says axionyms and hagiographic titles take lowercase initials (example `senhor doutor Joaquim da Silva`), while abbreviations in regulated form such as Sr. are written with capitals ([POR-S001](../sources.md#por-s001), Base XIX 1º f and 2º h).
- The Brazilian Senate manual says Dona and Seu are capitalized when a person is popularly known that way (example given: Dona Ivone Lara), that senhor/senhora should be abbreviated sr./sra. when followed by a name, that doutor should not be used as a form of address, and that dom is lowercase for church authorities and monarchs ([POR-S006](../sources.md#por-s006)).
- The two sources disagree in capitalization emphasis, and the Senate manual is house style, so no universal casing rule follows.

Confidence: **medium** for the casing facts, since they come from two official texts, and **low** for pt-PT because only the Acordo covers it.

## NER hypothesis

A policy that excludes Sr./Dr./Dra./Dom and keeps Dona/Seu only when fused with a name will yield fewer title-attachment boundary errors than one that always includes capitalized titles. Predicted failure: no difference once title policy is fixed in annotation.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: title-name pairs in abbreviated and written forms. Hard negatives: seu possessive, dona as common noun, dom as noun. Variation: capitalization, period, pt-BR versus pt-PT. Deferred pending policy decision.

## Implementation options

Baseline: current behavior. Option: honorific lexicon with possessive disambiguation. Tradeoff: seu ambiguity. Options only.
