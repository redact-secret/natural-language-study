# POR-003: Portuguese person names are sanctioned in several spellings, so accent and Unicode form must not define identity

## Question and scope

How do orthographic variation (António/Antônio), the right to keep a registered spelling, accent loss in noisy text and NFC/NFD forms affect matching and boundaries for Portuguese person names? Scope: pt-PT and pt-BR, Latn, edited text and informal or noisy text, PERSON. Excludes OCR error rates.

## Observation

- Base XI 3º of the Acordo lists António/Antônio as alternatives according to pronunciation (open versus closed vowel), so both are standard ([POR-S001](../sources.md#por-s001)). Base XXI lets a person keep the spelling adopted by custom or legal registration in a signature, so a name may differ from a general spelling rule.
- Precomposed and combining sequences (for example ã as U+00E3 or a + U+0303) are canonically equivalent; NFC and NFD exist ([POR-S012](../sources.md#por-s012)). Accent deletion (João to Joao) is not canonical equivalence.
- Duran et al. report 25,722 Portuguese word pairs that differ only by an accent and describe user-generated content as characterized by missing diacritics ([POR-S008](../sources.md#por-s008), abstract only). They study common words, not personal names.
- The suggested Fernando/Fernão variety contrast is not supported by any source read here: they are distinct given-name forms and no variety rule is claimed.

Confidence: **medium**. Standards and abstract are verified; no name-specific noise corpus was read.

## NER hypothesis

Canonical (NFC) lookup with a source-offset map will remove representation misses without changing identity, while accent deletion will remain a separate lossy transformation whose gain, if any, must be measured apart. Predicted failure: no recall gain for NFD input, or accent-stripped lookup raising false matches among accent-distinguished forms.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: the same name in NFC, NFD, pt-PT and pt-BR spellings, and a registered spelling. Hard negatives: accent-only word pairs. Variation: edited versus noisy. Deferred.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice **por-diacritics**: separate NFC/NFD from accent deletion; report recall change and false-match cases on accent pairs. Deferred; no measurement.

## Implementation options

Baseline: raw matching. Options: NFC normalization with offset mapping; accent-folded fallback kept separate. Tradeoff: folding raises ambiguity. Destination `fastner`; options only.

## Limitations and review

Date and details of national adoption (the 2009 timeline) were not verified from the text read. No corpus rate of accent loss in names. Only an abstract of the false-homograph paper was read. Examples are synthetic. Review: none recorded; author self-check only.
