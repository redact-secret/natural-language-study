# POR-006: Portuguese surnames and given names collide with common nouns, so capitalization and article cues carry the decision

## Question and scope

Which Portuguese personal names also have common-noun or verb readings, and what context separates them? Scope: pt-PT and pt-BR Latn text, PERSON versus common noun. Excludes place and organization readings except as contrasts.

## Observation

- Priberam lists silva as a feminine noun (bramble, a verse form, archaic forest) and as a verb form of silvar, with no proper-name sense in the entry as summarized, and rosa as a feminine noun (flower), masculine noun (colour), adjective and verb-form reading ([POR-S009](../sources.md#por-s009)). A dictionary lacking a proper-name sense does not show the word is not used as a name.
- The Acordo requires an initial capital for anthroponyms ([POR-S001](../sources.md#por-s001), Base XIX 2º a), so capitalization is a cue in edited text but fails in sentence-initial position and in lowercase or uppercase-only text.
- Pereira, Costa, Barros, Ribeiro, Flor, Estrela and Santos were not checked in a dictionary; they are hypotheses about ambiguity (pear tree, coast, and so on), not observations.

Confidence: **low**: two dictionary entries and a general orthographic requirement.

## NER hypothesis

Casing and article cues alone will leave residual errors on sentence-initial and case-stripped text, which context windows reduce; the lexicon of name-like common nouns should not be treated as a rejection list. Predicted failure: no residual errors after case-insensitive matching.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: the same word as name and noun in matched templates. Hard negatives: verb-form readings (silva, rosa). Variation: sentence position, case, pt-PT versus pt-BR. Deferred.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice **por-name-noun**: matched pairs under mixed, lower and upper case; report false positives and misses separately. Deferred.

## Implementation options

Baseline: current behavior. Option: add local context features. Tradeoff: more false positives on common nouns. Options only.
