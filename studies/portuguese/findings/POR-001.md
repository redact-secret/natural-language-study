# POR-001: Connectors da, de, do, das, dos and e can be name-internal or external, and spelling alone does not decide

## Question and scope

When do lowercase connectors (de, da, do, das, dos, e) between capitalized words belong to a Portuguese personal name, and when do they introduce an external complement? Scope: contemporary written pt-PT and pt-BR, Latn, edited prose, PERSON. Excludes organization names, place names and historical spelling.

## Observation

- The Acordo shows lowercase connectors inside capitalized anthroponyms in its own examples (Joaquim da Silva, Branca de Neve) and requires initial capitals for anthroponyms ([POR-S001](../sources.md#por-s001), Base XIX 1º f and 2º a). It states no standalone rule for particles inside surnames, so this is evidence of practice, not a stated norm.
- The Portuguese UD Bosque flat:name relation links proper nouns across an intervening preposition or conjunction (for example a name with DA, and an organization name with e) ([POR-S004](../sources.md#por-s004)); UD notes that many multiword proper nouns are not split ([POR-S005](../sources.md#por-s005)). This is a syntactic annotation choice, not a PERSON span rule.
- Consequence for recognition: the same surface sequence capital + da + capital can be one name or a possessor phrase, and the same da can precede a given name that is an external complement.

Confidence: **medium**. The practice is supported by a normative text and a treebank, but no source in this study quantifies how often each reading occurs, and the Acordo gives no explicit particle rule.

## NER hypothesis

A contextual boundary decision for connectors (look at the preceding given name and following capitalized token, and the left article) will recover multi-part names with fewer external-preposition attachments than a capitalization-run shortcut or a rule that always drops da/de/do. Predicted failure: the benefit disappears on possessive phrases like ex-02/ex-04 or coordinated people like ex-03.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: given name + da/de/do/das/dos + surname. Hard negatives: possessor phrases with the same connector, coordinated people with e, article-preceded names. Variation axes: pt-PT versus pt-BR, edited versus messages, lowercase or uppercase input. Destination `ner-evidence`; deferred pending reviewer and annotation-policy decision.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice **por-connectors**: hold names and templates fixed, vary the grammatical role of the connector, and compare contextual versus capitalization-run versus always-drop baselines on exact-span boundary errors (missing internal connector, added external connector). Destination `ner-eval`; deferred. No measurement exists.

## Implementation options

Baselines: current capitalization-run behavior; always drop connectors; contextual rule. Tradeoff: contextual rules need a name-position cue and may fail on all-lowercase text. Destination `fastner`; options only, no code inspected.

## Limitations and review

Connector behavior was inferred from one normative text and one syntactic treebank; there is no attested frequency, no register contrast, and examples are synthetic with author self-check only. Treebank examples were read through summaries. Review: none recorded; author self-check only.
