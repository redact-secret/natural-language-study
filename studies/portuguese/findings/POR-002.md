# POR-002: Preposition-article contractions and hyphenated enclitic or mesoclitic clitics create sub-token and hyphen boundaries that are not name boundaries

## Question and scope

How do contractions (do, da, no, na, pelo, pela) and hyphenated verb-clitic forms (dá-lhe, contar-lhe-ei) interact with adjacent names? Scope: contemporary pt-PT and pt-BR, Latn, edited prose and messages, PERSON. Excludes dialect pronunciation and the fused form à (crase) beyond the contrast below.

## Observation

- UD Portuguese analyzes contractions as multiword tokens: do = de + o, pelo = por + o, and says contractions are ubiquitous. It also gives enclisis (sentem-se) and mesoclisis (contar-lhe-ei) as multiword-token cases and says hyphenated compounds are currently treated inconsistently ([POR-S003](../sources.md#por-s003)).
- The Acordo's Base XVII prescribes a hyphen in enclisis and tmesis (amá-lo, dá-se, amá-lo-ei, enviar-lhe-emos) and with eis (eis-me) ([POR-S001](../sources.md#por-s001)).
- The hyphen in these forms joins a verb and a pronoun, not parts of a name. A tokenizer that treats a hyphen as a name-internal joiner (as in compound given names) will meet a different structure here.

Confidence: **medium**. The two sources agree on the forms; the study did not find an independent source for hyphen use inside Portuguese given names, so that side of the contrast is unsourced.

## NER hypothesis

Treating the hyphen as a name-internal joiner everywhere, or splitting every contraction before span prediction, will each produce boundary errors that a role-aware treatment avoids. Predicted failure: no difference on a matched set of hyphenated names versus verb-clitic forms.

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: contraction-name adjacency, hyphenated given names (needs a source), verb-clitic forms with sentence-initial capitals. Hard negatives: the same tokens without names. Variation: pt-PT enclisis frequency versus pt-BR proclisis preference (not verified here). Deferred.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice **por-contractions-clitics**: compare raw-token, UD-style split, and role-aware handling; report span errors and false PERSON candidates from capitalized verb forms. Deferred; no measurement.

## Implementation options

Baseline: current tokenization. Options: expose contraction splits as optional candidates while preserving source offsets; hyphen-role features. Tradeoff: splitting changes offsets. Destination `fastner`; options only.

## Limitations and review

The pt-PT versus pt-BR frequency claim about enclisis is a common observation not verified here and is not used. UD splits are not entity boundaries. Examples are synthetic and author-checked only. Review: none recorded; author self-check only.
