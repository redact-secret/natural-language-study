# TUR-005: Post-1934 surnames and titles around Turkish names: which neighbouring tokens belong to the PERSON span

## Question and scope

How are Turkish personal names ordered and surrounded by titles and honorific words, and which neighbouring tokens are plausible PERSON span members versus external context? Scope: contemporary standard written Turkish, Turkish citizens' naming as codified in the 1934 law and TDK usage; PERSON. Excludes non-Turkish naming communities within Turkey, minority naming, Ottoman nomenclature and any identity-linking.

## Observation

The 1934 Surname Law (no. 2525) required every citizen to take a surname, put the given name first and the surname after it in speech, writing and signatures, and barred surnames based on rank or position, tribe or foreign ethnic names [TUR-S013](../sources.md#tur-s013); this was read from a commercial reproduction through a summary, not the official text. TDK capitalization guidance, as summarized, capitalizes titles, respect words and ranks around names, giving `Kaymakam Erol Bey`, `Sayın Prof. Dr. Hasan Eren`, `Mustafa Efendi`, `Zeynep Hanım` and `Bay Ali Çiçekçi` [TUR-S002](../sources.md#tur-s002). So some honorifics precede the name (`Sayın`, `Dr.`, `Prof. Dr.`, `Bay`) and some follow it (`Bey`, `Hanım`, `Efendi`). The dictionary describes `bey` as a respect word used after male names, `hanım` as a title for girls and women, `sayın` as a respect marker placed before names, and `bay` as one placed before names or surnames [TUR-S014](../sources.md#tur-s014); the sense text read was truncated for `efendi`.

Not established here: whether a PERSON annotation policy should include any of these tokens, and the naming patterns `-oğlu` and `-gil` (background suspicion only; no source read).

Confidence **medium** for order and title placement; **low** for any span policy.

## NER hypothesis

Titles that precede a name and the honorifics `Bey/Hanım/Efendi` that follow it are distinguishable from the name proper by a closed-class lexicon plus position, so a policy that excludes titles and a policy that includes post-name honorifics differ in a predictable subset of mentions; cross-policy score differences will concentrate on those tokens. Challenge: no concentration of errors on them, or a corpus that annotates them inconsistently.
