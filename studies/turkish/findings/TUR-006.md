# TUR-006: Many Turkish given names are common nouns: capitalization and the apostrophe are the surface cues

## Question and scope

For words like `Gül`, `Deniz`, `Yıldız`, `Çiçek`, `Umut`, `Kaya`, `Demir`, `Aslan`, `Can` and `Ay`, which surface cues separate the PERSON reading from the common-noun reading, and when are they absent? Scope: standard written Turkish, PERSON versus common noun (plus the place and zodiac readings noted); a name list is not claimed to be complete or representative. Excludes frequency of names and the question of which names are most common.

## Observation

The TDK dictionary endpoint returns a common-noun entry for each of `gül`, `deniz`, `yıldız`, `çiçek`, `umut`, `kaya`, `demir`, `aslan`, `can` and `ay`, with senses such as rose (`gül`), sea, star or celebrity, flower, hope, rock, iron, lion, life, and moon or month [TUR-S014](../sources.md#tur-s014); only the first one or two senses were inspected and none of them was a given-name sense, so the dictionary does not settle whether a person-name sense is recorded. `Aslan` also has a separate proper-noun entry pointing to the zodiac sign. That these words are in use as given names is the author's background knowledge and is not documented by the sources read; the task treats it as an ambiguity hypothesis.

Surface cues: names are capitalized (TDK) [TUR-S002](../sources.md#tur-s002), common nouns are not except sentence-initially; a personal name takes an apostrophe before a suffix, a common noun does not [TUR-S001](../sources.md#tur-s001). So `Deniz'e` and `denize` differ in two ways. The cues disappear in ALL CAPS, lowercase noisy text, sentence-initial position, or when the apostrophe is omitted (see [TUR-002](TUR-002.md)).

Confidence **medium** for the cue inventory (normative rules plus dictionary); **low** for any claim about how often the names are used.

## NER hypothesis

A model that uses capitalization plus the apostrophe-before-suffix cue and a following honorific will separate person and common-noun readings on these words in edited text, and will fail mainly where the cues are absent. Challenge: the model resolves cue-free cases from semantics alone, or fails even when the cues are present, which would show the cues are not used.

## Evidence handoff

Contrast families: cue present or absent (apostrophe, capital, honorific), ALL CAPS, sentence-initial. Keep genuinely undecidable contexts in a separate adjudication bucket.

## Implementation options

Options: no change; cue features (apostrophe-suffix, capital, honorific) fed to the model; gazetteer prior (risk: biasing common nouns). Tradeoff: gazetteers help the person reading and hurt the noun reading.

Destination: `fastner`; proposed experiments only, **deferred**; no downstream code was inspected for this finding.
