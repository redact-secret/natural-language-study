# TUR-001: The apostrophe after a proper name marks a name/suffix boundary inside one whitespace token

## Question and scope

Where does a PERSON mention end when a case, possessive or predicate suffix follows a personal name in the same whitespace-delimited token? Scope: contemporary standard written Turkish in Turkey, Latin script, edited prose; PERSON, with place names used as a comparison. Excludes Ottoman Arabic-script text, dialects, speech and the omission of the apostrophe in noisy text (see [TUR-002](TUR-002.md)). The question matters because a whitespace tokenizer yields `Ayşe'ye` as one unit although the name is `Ayşe`.

## Observation

The Turkish Language Association (TDK) apostrophe page states, as relayed by a fetch summary, that possessive, case and predicate suffixes added to proper nouns are separated from them by an apostrophe, with examples such as `Kurtuluş Savaşı'nı` and `Atatürk'üm` [TUR-S001](../sources.md#tur-s001). In that reading, the apostrophe in a correctly spelled personal name plus suffix is an orthographic cue to a boundary that lies inside the token. Two UD treebanks confirm the form is frequent enough to be handled structurally: BOUN reports 2,148 word types mixing letters and punctuation with examples like `Türkiye'de` and `İstanbul'a`, and IMST reports 611 such types, mostly apostrophized proper names [TUR-S005](../sources.md#tur-s005), [TUR-S006](../sources.md#tur-s006). Both treebanks also contain multi-word tokens (3,374 and 1,639), i.e. a token with more than one syntactic word. UD word units are not PERSON spans, so this shows the segmentation problem exists, not what an NER gold policy should be. The Snowball Turkish stemmer treats the first apostrophe as a stem boundary (when at least two characters precede it) and warns about foreign names such as `o'connor` [TUR-S012](../sources.md#tur-s012), a deployed heuristic that is consistent with the rule and shows its limit.

Confidence **medium**: the rule is normative and consistent across sources, but every source was read only as a summary and no grammar book was read.

## NER hypothesis

Splitting at an apostrophe inside a capitalized token will give exact-span boundaries for correctly spelled name-plus-suffix tokens that a whitespace-only candidate generator misses, without changing which tokens are classified as PERSON. Prediction limited to boundary behavior, not classification. The hypothesis is challenged if apostrophe-aware candidates do not improve exact-span recall on matched tokens, or if they lower precision on apostrophes that do not follow a name (`ex-03` title case, abbreviations such as `TBMM'nin`, numbers, foreign names with an internal apostrophe).

## Evidence handoff

Positive cases: personal given names and full names plus single case, possessive and predicate suffixes. Hard negatives: place and organization names with apostrophes, title-plus-apostrophe (`ex-03`), abbreviations and numbers, and non-Turkish names containing an apostrophe. Contrast families: apostrophe type (U+0027 versus U+2019; unsourced, to be checked), suffix chains, name-final vowel versus consonant. Variation axes: edited versus noisy text.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice `tr-apostrophe-suffix`: apostrophe-aware versus whitespace-only candidate generation over identical tokens, scored on exact boundary match, with separate counts for person versus place/organization. Question: does boundary recall rise without a rise in false boundaries on title and abbreviation apostrophes?

Destination: `ner-eval`; no issue exists. Disposition: **deferred** until reviewed evidence exists. Hold fixed the evidence snapshot, model, decoding and scoring contract; report slice denominators, exact-span precision/recall and boundary-error types. No threshold is proposed.

## Implementation options

Options: (a) no change baseline; (b) split a token at its first apostrophe as a feature or candidate edge; (c) a learned character-level boundary model; (d) morphological analysis. Tradeoff: (b) is cheap but fails the title case and foreign-name apostrophes; (d) needs a Turkish analyzer not examined here.

Destination: `fastner`; proposed experiments only, **deferred**; no downstream code was inspected for this finding.
