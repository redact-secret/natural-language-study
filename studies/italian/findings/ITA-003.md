# ITA-003: Titles and surname-first registry order sit outside or around the name span and depend on register

## Question and scope

How do Italian titles (Sig., Sig.ra, Dott., Dott.ssa, Avv., Prof., Ing., On.) and the surname-first order used by public administration affect which tokens form a PERSON mention? Scope: it-IT, edited correspondence and registry-like text, PERSON. Exclusions: Ing. and On. beyond what the sources cover, regional forms, religious titles.

## Observation

Crusca states that in Italian the given name normally precedes the surname and that the inverse order is left mostly to public-administration language ([ITA-S001](../sources.md#ita-s001), Posizione). Crusca's answer on salutations says sig./sig.ra, dott./dott.ssa and prof. may be abbreviated or written in full and that capitalization of professional titles is optional except for high offices, and the S001 answer also writes `Avv. Mario Rossi` with an abbreviated title before the name ([ITA-S002](../sources.md#ita-s002); [ITA-S001](../sources.md#ita-s001), Grafia). Treccani's capitalization guide treats titles and offices under Cariche e titoli ([ITA-S007](../sources.md#ita-s007)). The consulted sources do not mention Ing. or On., nor ALL-CAPS surname-first lists such as ROSSI Mario; those are unsourced here and recorded only as gaps. Confidence medium; the claims are advisory, not statistics.

## NER hypothesis

Treating the title as a left-context feature and the surname-first pair as one span is expected to recover more mentions than a rule keyed only to `Given Surname` order. Refuted if surname-first lists are already handled equally by a capitalization-run baseline, or if title-as-feature does not lower sentence-boundary errors after `Dott.ssa.`-style abbreviations.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: titles with period, without period, full word; surname-first forms; initial-only mentions. Hard negatives: titles with no name, office nouns.
