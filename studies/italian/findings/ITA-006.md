# ITA-006: Italian naming law and practice: up to three given names, wives keep birth surname with an optional addition, and children may carry two surnames

## Question and scope

How do statute and Crusca guidance bear on multiple given names, married women's surnames and double surnames for children, and what do they imply for mention span? Scope: it-IT, PERSON, registry-like text. Exclusions: foreign-law names, legal advice, individual cases, historical custom.

## Observation

D.P.R. 396/2000 art. 35 permits one name or several names, not more than three ([ITA-S010](../sources.md#ita-s010)). Art. 143 bis c.c. says the wife adds her husband's surname to her own ([ITA-S009](../sources.md#ita-s009)); Crusca describes the chronological order inherited surname then acquired surname ([ITA-S001](../sources.md#ita-s001), Posizione). Whether registries record the addition is not established by the sources read; claims that Italian identity documents show only the birth surname appeared in search snippets and are unverified. A scholarly commentary reports that sentenza n. 131/2022 declared art. 262 c.c. unconstitutional insofar as the father's surname was automatic when both parents recognized the child, and speaks of both parents' surnames in the order agreed by them ([ITA-S011](../sources.md#ita-s011)). The commentary, not the ruling, was read. Whether `Gian Luca`, `Gianluca` and `Maria Chiara` are one given name or two is unsourced here. Confidence low: statute extracts and a secondary commentary only.

## NER hypothesis

Allowing two or three given-name tokens and one or two surname tokens inside a PERSON span, without trying to infer which is birth or acquired, will recover more full names than a fixed two-token `Given Surname` pattern. Refuted if a two-token baseline with context handles these forms equally on matched negatives.
