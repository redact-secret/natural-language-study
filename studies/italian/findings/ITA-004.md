# ITA-004: Accents, apostrophes and Unicode form: Niccolò and Nicolò coexist, and U+0027 versus U+2019 and NFC versus NFD change strings without changing the name

## Question and scope

Which representation variants of Italian accented and apostrophized names should a recognizer treat as the same string, and which differences are real spelling choices? Scope: it-IT Latin script, PERSON, edited and noisy text. Exclusions: transliteration from other scripts, speech, optical error rates.

## Observation

Crusca's answer, republished by Linkiesta, says Niccolò and Nicolò are both correct and have coexisted for centuries ([ITA-S008](../sources.md#ita-s008)). The apostrophe is the elision mark in Italian ([ITA-S004](../sources.md#ita-s004)). The Unicode Standard prefers U+2019 as the apostrophe character, though U+0027 is on most keyboards ([ITA-S015](../sources.md#ita-s015), 6.2.7), so both forms are legitimate encodings of the same elision. Precomposed and decomposed accented letters are canonically equivalent under NFC and NFD ([ITA-S014](../sources.md#ita-s014)). Italian civil registry commentary says foreign names keep their original diacritics, extended Italian alphabet letters including J K X Y W ([ITA-S016](../sources.md#ita-s016)); that is secondary and dated 2014. No consulted source measures how often accents are dropped in noisy Italian text; that loss is an author hypothesis, not an observation. Confidence medium: standards are clear, name-spelling claims rest on a republished consultation.

## NER hypothesis

Matching that is invariant to NFC/NFD and to U+0027/U+2019, but does not equate accent-stripped or Niccolò/Nicolò forms by default, will keep recall for encoding noise without conflating distinct names. Refuted if the Niccolò/Nicolò or accent-deleted forms lose recall under exact-form lexicons, or if canonical normalization changes span offsets that downstream scoring cannot map.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: NFC, NFD, both apostrophes, accented and deleted-accent variants of the same fictional name. Hard negatives: distinct name pairs such as Niccolò/Nicolò kept separate. Always keep the original string and a named transformation. Destination: `ner-evidence`; no issue opened. Proposal is ready for owner review after Italian-language review of examples.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice `ita-encoding-variants`: same names across four encoding variants. Separate recall under encoding equivalence from false merges under accent deletion. Destination: `ner-eval`; a proposed slice name, not a registered dimension. No measurement exists.
