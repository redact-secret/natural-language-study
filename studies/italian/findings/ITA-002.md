# ITA-002: Surname particles may be lowercase (da Vinci) or capitalized (De Luca, D'Eredità): case cannot decide membership

## Question and scope

Do Italian personal names keep a particle inside the surname span regardless of case, and how does register change casing? Scope: it-IT written text, PERSON, edited prose and registry-like lists. Exclusions: noble-title status, pronunciation, dialect, specific individual spellings.

## Observation

Caffarelli (Crusca) says the preposition in a name such as Leonardo da Vinci is written in lowercase, while a particle that belongs to the surname itself is capitalized, as in D'Eredità ([ITA-S001](../sources.md#ita-s001), Grafia). Treccani's surname typology lists patronymics formed with prepositions, with Di Pietro and De Maria as examples ([ITA-S006](../sources.md#ita-s006)). Treccani's capitalization guide requires capitals for surnames but the extract does not discuss particles ([ITA-S007](../sources.md#ita-s007)). Crusca's capitalization advice rests on the principle that a word naming a unique individual takes a capital ([ITA-S003](../sources.md#ita-s003)). Taken together, the sources describe both a lowercase pattern for origin phrases and a capitalized pattern for particles fused into the surname. They do not give a rule for which spelling an individual uses, so individual registry spelling controls. Alphabetization treats apostrophes and spaces differently in print tradition and in digital systems ([ITA-S001](../sources.md#ita-s001), Ordine alfabetico), so De Luca, DeLuca and D'Auria sort differently by system. Confidence medium: one Crusca answer plus typology; no corpus.

## NER hypothesis

Using case-robust, context-based membership for De/Di/Della/Da will reduce omission of internal particles compared with a rule that requires capitalization or drops lowercase particles, without adding external `di/del/della` phrases (example-04, example-06 contrast). Refuted if external function words are absorbed at a higher rate than the omissions saved.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: capitalized and lowercase particles, ALL-CAPS and mixed-case lists. Hard negatives: external di/della/del before a given name or a common noun (for example `la casa della rocca` with a common noun, and `la casa di Luca` with a given name). Source or register must be recorded rather than normalized; individual spellings need registry verification. Destination: `ner-evidence`; no issue opened. Proposal is ready for owner review after Italian-language review of examples.

## Evaluation handoff

Proposed slice `ita-particle-case`: hold the name inventory and context fixed, vary only particle case and ALL-CAPS. Compare internal-particle omission and external-function-word inclusion separately. Destination: `ner-eval`; a proposed slice name, not a registered dimension. No measurement exists.

## Implementation options

Options: raw casing with context features; case-folded particle lookup that keeps original coordinates; baseline title-case runs. Do not rewrite stored names to a canonical case. Destination: `fastner`; options only, no code inspected or changed for this finding.

## Limitations and review

The lowercase pattern in [ITA-S001](../sources.md#ita-s001) comes from one named example set; the sources do not show how common De/de or Di/di variants are in registries. Example-03 and example-05 are perturbations. ALL-CAPS behavior is not covered by any consulted source. Author self-check only; no independent or qualified language review is recorded, so nothing here is marked reviewed.
