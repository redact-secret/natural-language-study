# ITA-005: Italian name homographs: given names, surnames, common nouns, places and demonyms overlap, and case helps only mid-sentence

## Question and scope

Which surface forms such as Rosa, Fiore, Gioia, Marino, Conte, Re, Bianchi, Pace, Giorgio, Valentino, Romano and Fiorentino have person and non-person readings, and what context separates them? Scope: it-IT, PERSON versus common noun, place and demonym, edited prose and constructed contrasts. Exclusions: organization names, brand names beyond a single example, frequency.

## Observation

Treccani's surname typology states that surnames derive from personal names, nicknames, ethnic and place terms and occupations, and that many forms are ambiguous between place name and personal name ([ITA-S006](../sources.md#ita-s006)). Crusca states that ethnic adjectives are normally lowercase and that a common noun can be capitalized when it designates a specific individual ([ITA-S003](../sources.md#ita-s003)); Treccani's guide requires capitals for proper names and treats offices and titles separately ([ITA-S007](../sources.md#ita-s007)). So mid-sentence capitalization separates Rosa the name from rosa the flower, but sentence-initial position, headlines, ALL-CAPS and capitalized offices (Conte, Re as titles) remove or blur that cue. The consulted sources give typology and capitalization principles; they do not list these particular homographs or give frequencies, so each pair below is the author's example. Confidence medium for the typology, low for any statement about how often each reading occurs.

## NER hypothesis

A model using preceding title or determiner and sentence position will separate person and non-person readings of these homographs better than a lexicon of name-like words alone. Refuted if a name list alone matches the context-sensitive model on matched negatives.

## Evidence handoff

Positive: each homograph as a given name or surname in subject, object and post-title positions. Hard negatives: same forms as lowercase common nouns or adjectives. Include sentence-initial and all-caps cases; no frequencies assumed. Destination: `ner-evidence`; no issue opened. Proposal is ready for owner review after Italian-language review of examples.
